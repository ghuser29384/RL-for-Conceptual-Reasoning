# Q-006A calibration and non-final item-screening protocol

**Status:** Screening protocol prepared; no calibration or protected pilot item has been selected, frozen, assigned, distributed, or rated.  
**Authorization:** Q-006A permits public non-protected calibration screening and private non-final pilot-item screening only.  
**Does not authorize:** protected-manifest freeze, participant selection, assignment, calibration work, rating work, adjudication, publication of protected text, or Phase 2.

## A. Public calibration-candidate screening

### Target

Screen candidates for a proposed calibration packet of:

- 2 public, non-protected positions;
- 4 critiques per position;
- 8 critiques total; and
- use by all six eventual core raters and both eventual adjudicators before protected production work.

Screening is not selection. The exact items and qualification rule remain pending Q-006B.

### Required issue coverage

Across the eight critiques, the candidate packet should cover:

1. interpretation ambiguity in the position or critique;
2. a critique that may already be “priced in” to the position;
3. strength–centrality allocation ambiguity;
4. a low-clarity critique;
5. a correctness-sensitive claim for which practical verification matters;
6. a specific but low-centrality objection;
7. dead weight versus an unsuccessful substantive argument; and
8. single issue versus multiple independent issues.

One critique may cover more than one issue, but the packet should not depend on one item for most of the coverage.

### Eligibility gates

A calibration candidate must:

- be lawfully public and non-protected;
- have a stable public source or be part of Metaphilosophy’s public synthetic release;
- have no participant-specific or protected label attached;
- contain enough context for a careful reader to identify the intended conclusion;
- avoid requiring specialized factual knowledge unavailable in the packet, unless verification is itself the intended calibration issue;
- be suitable for redistribution in the calibration packet;
- contain no personal or sensitive information; and
- remain excluded from the 48-critique production outcomes.

### Screening fields

For each candidate, record privately or in a public-safe public-candidate worksheet:

- candidate ID and version;
- public source and rights basis;
- topic family;
- position and critique word counts;
- intended rubric issue coverage;
- ambiguity and context notes;
- verification burden;
- likely time burden;
- source/style cue risk;
- reason for provisional inclusion or exclusion; and
- reviewer exposure status.

### Considerations dossier

The later packet should reveal a rubric-based considerations dossier after every participant locks an initial calibration rating. The dossier may identify relevant interpretations, verification facts, and rubric considerations. It must not require conformity to a single unquestionable gold vector.

Initial calibration ratings remain preserved. Discussion or remediation must use object-level reasons. No calibration rating earns an honorarium unit under the current plan.

## B. Private non-final pilot-item screening

### Target structure

Screen a candidate pool capable of supporting:

- 12 positions;
- 6 topic families with 2 positions each;
- a preferred 6/6 crossing between public-synthetic and protected-public-domain-derived source classes;
- at least 8 candidate critiques per position; and
- 4 provisionally selected critiques per position.

The preferred crossing is a screening target, not a frozen quota. If independent source-fidelity or ambiguity review makes it infeasible, document the deviation for Q-006B rather than weakening review gates.

### Position gates

A position may be provisionally included only when:

- its source and authorship record is complete;
- rights review permits the intended controlled research use;
- its intended conclusion and relevant context can be stated without changing the substantive text;
- independent source-fidelity review passes for public-domain-derived material;
- ambiguity and scope risks are understood and not disqualifying;
- it fits one approved proposed topic family;
- it has at least eight viable critique candidates or a documented acquisition path to eight;
- no known reviewer conflict or prior-label exposure makes the preferred assignment graph infeasible; and
- it does not duplicate another position’s substantive test function without a documented reason.

### Critique gates

A critique candidate must have:

- a controlled critique ID and version;
- a source or generation record;
- rights and provenance status;
- the exact unchanged candidate text;
- word and sentence counts;
- formatting and citation diagnostics;
- attack-family classification;
- source/style cue risk;
- acquisition-judge mean and disagreement commitments, when used;
- source-fidelity, ambiguity, and scope review;
- likely correctness-verification burden; and
- a provisional inclusion or exclusion reason.

Acquisition-judge outputs are selection signals, not labels. They remain hidden from human raters.

### Provisional four-critique objective

For each position, the provisional set should ordinarily include:

- one candidate with high acquisition-judge mean and low disagreement;
- one plausible candidate with low mean and low disagreement;
- two candidates with high disagreement or meaningfully distinct attack families; and
- a set whose apparent quality is not trivially predictable from length, formatting, citation style, source, model identity, or tone.

The text must not be substantively rewritten merely to conceal its source. Residual source/style cue risk should be recorded and later reported as a limitation or sensitivity analysis.

### Exclusion reasons

Use a controlled reason code, including:

- rights unclear or unsuitable;
- source fidelity failed;
- intended conclusion too ambiguous;
- insufficient context;
- scope mismatch;
- duplicate substantive test function;
- critique too generic to evaluate;
- verification burden impractical;
- source/style confound excessive;
- candidate-pool arithmetic incomplete;
- assignment conflict or exposure risk;
- topic/source matrix imbalance; or
- other documented reason.

Exclusion is non-punitive and does not imply the underlying philosophical view is false.

## C. Freeze boundary

Q-006A screening may produce provisional records and exclusion rationales. It may not produce the final protected manifest.

A Q-006B freeze must separately approve:

- the exact 12 positions and 48 critiques;
- all item versions;
- topic and source allocation;
- context cards;
- rights, provenance, fidelity, ambiguity, and exposure records;
- candidate-selection and exclusion-ledger hashes;
- rubric and analysis-policy hashes;
- the assignment graph; and
- the regeneration rule for any later text change.

A substantive change after freeze creates a new version and invalidates affected assignment, packet, exposure, and downstream commitments.

## D. Public-output boundary

Before Q-006B, public reporting may contain only:

- aggregate numbers screened, provisionally included, and excluded;
- reason-code counts;
- topic/source coverage counts;
- approved protocol versions; and
- cryptographic commitments to private controlled ledgers.

It must not disclose protected text, controlled item IDs, acquisition-judge outputs, provisional strata, participant identities, conflict or exposure mappings, assignments, or labels.
