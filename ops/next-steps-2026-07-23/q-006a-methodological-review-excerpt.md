# Metaphilosophy 48-critique pilot — bounded methodological-review excerpt

**Version:** Q-006A consultation excerpt v1, 2026-08-01  
**Status:** non-binding consultation material  
**Contains:** no protected item, participant, assignment, rating, case, or payment data  
**Requested review:** approximately 20 minutes asynchronously or one 30-minute call  
**Participation does not imply endorsement. Quotation or attribution requires separate permission.**

## Research question

Can a small, auditable, multi-rater pilot determine whether expert ratings of philosophical critiques are sufficiently interpretable, reliable, and operationally tractable to justify a larger dataset programme?

The pilot is not designed to establish a generally authoritative theory of philosophical quality, settle substantive philosophical disputes, compare broad source or model classes causally, or justify Phase 2 automatically.

## Proposed sample and assignment

- 12 philosophical positions
- 4 sibling critiques per position
- 48 critiques total
- 2 independent blind initial ratings per critique
- 96 accepted initial ratings at completion
- 6 early-career core raters
- 2 dedicated adjudicators
- position is the assignment, comparison, and resampling cluster

The preferred topic matrix contains two positions in each of:

1. normative ethics;
2. political philosophy;
3. epistemology and philosophy of science;
4. philosophy of mind and AI consciousness;
5. decision theory and social choice; and
6. metaphilosophy and AI governance.

The preferred source crossing contains six public-synthetic positions receiving new expert ratings and six protected public-domain-derived positions, with one position from each source class in every topic family.

The anonymous assignment graph uses twelve distinct rater pairs. Each core rater receives four positions, four distinct partners, and four topic families. A participant may be mapped only to topic families separately approved as within their competence. Authorship conflict and prior exposure are hard exclusions. If no mapping satisfies all constraints, the system produces no assignment rather than weakening a constraint.

## Candidate acquisition and item controls

For each position, the proposal is to collect at least eight candidate critiques and select four only after freezing:

- word and sentence counts;
- formatting and citation cues;
- source and author/model identity;
- acquisition-judge means and disagreement;
- attack-family tags; and
- source-style-cue risk.

Acquisition scores are sampling signals, not labels. Critiques are not substantively rewritten merely to conceal their source. Residual source identifiability is documented and analyzed only after initial ratings lock.

## Rating rubric

Each critique receives scores from 0 to 1 on:

1. **centrality** — how much the critique targets a load-bearing part of the position;
2. **strength** — how strongly the critique undermines the targeted claim, conditional on engaging it correctly;
3. **correctness** — factual and inferential correctness;
4. **clarity** — how clearly the critique can be understood and assessed;
5. **dead weight** — irrelevant, redundant, or non-contributory material;
6. **single issue** — whether the critique remains focused enough to evaluate as one objection; and
7. **overall** — overall quality as a critique of the position.

Substantive impact is analyzed through `strength × centrality`; neither component alone is treated as decisive.

The rater also records a concise rationale, confidence, elapsed time, insufficient-context status, verification status, and item-integrity flags.

## Blindness

Before all four sibling critiques for a position are locked, raters do not receive:

- source class or identity;
- author or model identity;
- acquisition-judge scores or provisional strata;
- the paired rater’s identity or ratings;
- aggregate human ratings or labels; or
- adjudication status.

Every initial record is immutable. Any later rerating is append-only, predecessor-linked, operator-assigned, and supported by an object-level reason.

## Calibration proposal

All six core raters and both adjudicators independently rate the same eight public, non-protected calibration critiques across two positions.

Initial calibration ratings are preserved. Discussion uses a considerations dossier rather than an unquestionable answer key. The calibration set is excluded from pilot outcomes and currently earns zero honorarium units. The exact examples, remediation rule, and numerical qualification threshold remain unapproved.

## Candidate review routes

The following are consultation candidates, not approved operational policy:

- overall-score gap at least 0.30;
- `strength × centrality` gap at least 0.30;
- correctness gap at least 0.35;
- clarity gap at least 0.35;
- either rater assigns clarity below 0.5;
- either rater reports unresolved correctness-sensitive verification;
- either rater reports insufficient context or a source-fidelity, ambiguity, scope, or leakage concern.

The checked-in project policy has zero operative routes. No case opens until a later Q-006B decision approves a versioned policy.

## Adjudication proposal

One critique-level case opens for all operative routes applying to that critique. The case preserves both initial ratings and is assigned only to a qualified, conflict-free dedicated adjudicator with approved topic coverage.

The adjudicator may:

- clarify the position or critique;
- identify factual or interpretive considerations;
- document why a route is resolved or remains unresolved; and
- invite an original rater to consider an object-level reason under a separately authorized discussion process.

The adjudicator may not:

- create, edit, average, or replace a rating;
- declare one rater the winner;
- impose a consensus score;
- use majority vote; or
- pressure either rater to converge.

A case closes as resolved without rerating, resolved after a valid append-only rerating by an original rater, or explicitly unresolved. The final label snapshot preserves both initial ratings, the latest accepted rating from each original rater, and residual disagreement; it creates no synthetic consensus score.

## Analysis and small-sample safeguards

The proposed report contains:

- all twelve position-level result blocks;
- separate immutable-initial and latest-accepted results;
- within-position weighted ordering agreement;
- dimension-level absolute rater differences;
- interval-distance reliability diagnostics;
- accepted-rating-time distributions;
- candidate and operative route counts;
- consensus-score spread by position as a descriptive diagnostic only; and
- leave-one-position-out sensitivity ranges using position as the omitted unit.

Reliability coefficients, model comparisons, source/topic contrasts, and subgroup effects remain exploratory. The pilot cannot claim general superiority of a source class, model family, topic, or individual rater.

## Provisional scale-readiness candidates

The following are questions for review rather than automatic gates:

- median accepted-rating time no more than 15 minutes;
- weighted within-position pairwise agreement at least 0.75 among critique pairs whose mean overall-score gap is at least 0.20;
- at least 9 of 12 positions with consensus overall-score spread at least 0.30; and
- no more than 25% of critiques unresolved because of context, source fidelity, ambiguity, or scope after review.

Even if later approved and met, these values would not activate Phase 2 automatically. Expansion still requires a separate owner decision plus external funding or documented complete qualified-volunteer capacity.
