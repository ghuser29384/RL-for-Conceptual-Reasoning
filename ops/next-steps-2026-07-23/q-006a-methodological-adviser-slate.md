## Selection criteria

Candidates were prioritized for one or more of the following:

- methodology for evaluating philosophical arguments or philosophical judgment;
- experimental-philosophy, measurement, or disagreement expertise;
- argumentation, logic, and epistemic-methodology expertise;
- philosophy of mind and AI-consciousness expertise relevant to one pilot topic family;
- seniority sufficient for bounded protocol criticism rather than bulk rating; and
- a public institutional page and professional contact route that can be reverified immediately before any separately authorized send.

No candidate is represented as interested, available, affiliated with, or supportive of Metaphilosophy.

## Recommended outreach order

### Wave 1

#### 1. Catarina Dutilh Novaes

**Public professional source:** https://research.vu.nl/en/persons/catarina-dutilh-novaes  
**Professional address to reverify before send:** c.dutilhnovaes@vu.nl  
**Methodological fit:** argumentation, logic, reasoning practices, and the social dimensions of inquiry. Particularly well placed to assess whether the seven-dimensional rubric separates distinct argumentative defects, whether sibling-critique comparison creates undesirable anchoring, and whether unresolved disagreement is represented honestly.

**Bounded questions to prioritize:**

1. Does the rubric allocate centrality, strength, correctness, clarity, dead weight, single issue, and overall in a conceptually coherent way?
2. Does the proposed `strength × centrality` treatment obscure any important failure mode?
3. Does the case-closure design preserve disagreement without collapsing into either forced convergence or unstructured pluralism?

#### 2. Edouard Machery

**Public professional source:** https://www.hps.pitt.edu/people/edouard-machery  
**Professional address to reverify before send:** machery@pitt.edu  
**Methodological fit:** experimental philosophy, philosophy of science, construct validity, and the interpretation of variation in philosophical judgment. Particularly well placed to assess calibration, the meaning of inter-rater disagreement, source/style confounding, and the inferential limits of a twelve-position pilot.

**Bounded questions to prioritize:**

1. Are the proposed calibration and qualification procedures measuring philosophical competence rather than conformity to an internal answer key?
2. Which agreement statistics and small-sample safeguards are defensible for twelve position clusters?
3. What claims about source classes, topic families, or rater reliability must be prohibited at this sample size?
