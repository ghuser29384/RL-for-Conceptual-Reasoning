### Rater A journey

1. Redeem invitation on desktop.
2. Explain the position–critique unit and the seven dimensions in the participant's own words.
3. Rate all four sibling critiques.
4. Confirm `strength × centrality` and low-clarity treatment.
5. Save at least one incomplete draft and observe autosave.
6. Close the browser, reopen, and verify exact recovery.
7. Submit all four ratings and inspect the immutable receipt.
8. Request one object-level correction.
9. Operator reviews the request and, if the exercise requires it, creates a predecessor-linked rerating without overwriting the initial ratings.
10. Complete the debrief.

1. Redeem invitation in a narrow/mobile viewport.
2. Explain the position–critique unit and the seven dimensions in the participant's own words.
3. Rate all four sibling critiques.
4. Trigger one controlled recoverable failed request without exposing data or corrupting state.
5. Verify retry, autosave, and no duplicate write.
6. Submit all four ratings and inspect the immutable receipt.
7. Request withdrawal and verify the visible locked/withdrawn state while the private immutable history remains intact.
8. Complete the debrief.

### Immediate stop conditions

Stop the session, revoke outstanding access, and preserve evidence if any of the following occurs:

- access to another identity, assignment, draft, rating, receipt, or participant state;
- source, authorship, paired-rater score, provisional label, or adjudication-state leakage;
- lost or silently overwritten draft content;
- duplicate or non-idempotent submission;
- receipt mismatch or missing immutable initial ratings;
- inability to revoke or replace compromised access;
- broken event-chain or restore invariant;
- real, protected, or unpublished research content appears;
- `research_ratings_authorized` is not exactly false;
- the participant cannot distinguish the core rubric dimensions after reasonable orientation;
- any P0/P1 security, privacy, integrity, or comprehension defect.

## 8. Debrief questionnaire

### Rubric comprehension

1. In your own words, what is centrality measuring?
2. In your own words, what is strength measuring?
3. Why can centrality and strength be ambiguous individually while their product remains important?
4. What should happen to the other component ratings when clarity is below 0.5?
5. Why are initial ratings preserved after a later reconsideration?
6. What kinds of facts or claims should be verified before assigning correctness?

### Workflow usability

7. At any point, were you unsure what to do next?
8. Did autosave status accurately match what you believed had been saved?
9. After closing and reopening, was every field restored exactly as expected?
10. Was the locked state after submission clear?
11. Was the correction or withdrawal path understandable?
12. Did any text suggest that your synthetic scores would be used as research data?
13. Did you see any source, author, other-rater, aggregate, provisional-label, or adjudication information that should have been hidden?
14. What was the most confusing or cognitively burdensome part of the interface?
15. What single change would most improve the experience for an expert rater?
