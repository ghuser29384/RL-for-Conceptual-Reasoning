## Subject line

Request for 20-minute methodological review of a philosophy-rating pilot

## Common initial message

Dear Professor [SURNAME],

I’m developing Metaphilosophy, a project testing whether carefully structured expert ratings of philosophical critiques can produce useful training and evaluation data for AI systems doing philosophy.

Before recruiting raters or freezing any pilot items, I’m seeking a small amount of bounded methodological criticism from two to four senior researchers. The proposed pilot contains 12 philosophical positions, four critiques per position, and two independent ratings per critique. It uses a seven-dimensional rubric, keeps source and model identity hidden during initial rating, preserves original ratings after any reconsideration, and allows disagreement to remain explicitly unresolved rather than forcing a consensus score.

Would you be willing to spend approximately 20 minutes reviewing a short protocol excerpt and answering three focused questions? A 30-minute call is also possible, but asynchronous comments would be fully sufficient. I am not asking for bulk rating, item writing, routine adjudication, fundraising, a continuing advisory role, or endorsement.

The questions I would especially value from you are:

[CANDIDATE-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS]

I would send only a short, non-protected protocol excerpt. No participant data or hidden evaluation items would be included. Any quotation or public attribution would require your separate permission, and participation would not be presented as endorsement.

Thank you for considering this.

Best,
Ellen Sun
Project owner, Metaphilosophy
https://www.metaphilosophy.org

## Candidate-specific question blocks

### Catarina Dutilh Novaes

1. Does the rubric allocate centrality, strength, correctness, clarity, dead weight, single issue, and overall in a conceptually coherent way?
2. Does the proposed use of `strength × centrality` obscure an important argumentative failure mode?
3. Does the case-closure design preserve disagreement without collapsing into either forced convergence or unstructured pluralism?

### Edouard Machery

1. Are the proposed calibration and qualification procedures measuring philosophical competence rather than conformity to an internal answer key?
2. Which agreement statistics and small-sample safeguards are defensible for twelve position clusters?
3. What claims about source classes, topic families, or rater reliability must be prohibited at this sample size?

### Joshua Knobe

1. Does the design support meaningful conclusions about rating reliability without conflating disagreement with error?
2. Are the proposed public calibration examples and considerations dossiers likely to induce demand effects or conformity?
3. What minimum evidence would justify expanding beyond the pilot?

### Eric Schwitzgebel

1. Does the philosophy-of-mind and AI-consciousness topic family require special item or expertise controls?
2. Is the explicit-unresolved closure state sufficiently informative and non-coercive?
3. Could the protocol create a false impression of authoritative philosophical consensus, and how should that risk be reduced?
