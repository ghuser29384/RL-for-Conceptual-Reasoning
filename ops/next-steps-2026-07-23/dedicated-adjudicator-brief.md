## Role

Metaphilosophy’s 48-critique pilot proposes two dedicated adjudicators who are separate from the six core raters. Their function is to review only cases opened by an explicitly approved operative policy after accepted initial ratings exist.

The role is not to decide which rater is “right,” replace the two ratings with an adjudicator score, force convergence, or create a philosophical consensus that the evidence does not support. The role is to:

- identify the object-level considerations underlying a routed disagreement or item/evidence issue;
- determine whether each operative route is resolved or remains unresolved;
- verify that any later rater reconsideration follows the approved append-only procedure;
- preserve minority or alternative interpretations;
- close the required case through a versioned, independently quality-controlled record; and
- sign the final distribution-preserving label snapshot when it accurately represents the initial, latest-accepted, resolved, and unresolved records.

This is a bounded volunteer research contribution with limited honoraria. It is not represented as employment, a per-case wage, or full compensation for professional time.

## Recommended eligibility

A candidate should normally:

- be a philosophy PhD student, advanced research-degree student, recent philosophy PhD, or comparably qualified researcher;
- demonstrate careful object-level philosophical judgment and the ability to distinguish interpretation, correctness, context, and substantive disagreement;
- have separately approved topic-family coverage for every assigned case;
- complete the approved calibration;
- have no position or critique conflict, authorship or substantive-editing involvement, or prior protected-label exposure for assigned cases;
- not be one of the two initial raters for an assigned case;
- agree to the blindness, confidentiality, no-score-imposition, no-convergence-pressure, revision, audit, and data-handling rules; and
- be available for the bounded post-rating closure period.

Prestige, seniority, or institutional affiliation does not substitute for object-level competence. Senior methodological advisers are not presumed to be adjudicators.

## Case assignment

One critique-level case is created when at least one explicitly approved operative route applies. Several routes for the same critique remain one case.

Cases are assigned deterministically to the least-loaded eligible adjudicator, with a secret-seed hash tiebreak. Topic coverage, conflict, prior exposure, and separation from the original raters are hard constraints. Across two adjudicators, assignment counts differ by at most one where feasible.

If neither adjudicator is eligible, no assignment is created. The project must replace or expand the approved roster or obtain a versioned methodological change; it cannot silently relax a constraint.

The existence of a case packet is not permission to begin. Distribution and adjudication work require separate authorization.

## What the adjudicator sees

The private case packet may contain:

- the exact position and critique identifiers and frozen versions;
- the two preserved accepted initial rating records under anonymous rater labels;
- the explicitly operative routes and their observed details;
- relevant item, context, or verification records permitted by the approved policy;
- the case, initial-snapshot, and policy commitments; and
- the required response schema.

It should not contain unnecessary personal information, payment data, unapproved source identity, model-acquisition scores, participant contact information, or another adjudicator’s draft resolution.

The adjudicator must not attempt to identify the anonymous initial raters, search for hidden source information, compare case packets outside the approved process, or disclose protected content.

## Object-level review

The adjudicator records considerations rather than imposed answers. Relevant considerations may include:

- alternative plausible interpretations of the position or critique;
- whether a criticism is already “priced in” to the position;
- how much detail the critique needs to establish its point;
- whether a correctness-sensitive claim has been practically verified;
- whether low clarity makes non-clarity dimensions unreliable;
- whether the item lacks enough context for stable evaluation;
- whether a source-fidelity, ambiguity, scope, or leakage issue affects use of the item; and
- whether the disagreement is substantive and should remain unresolved.

The adjudicator must not direct a rater to change a score merely because it differs from another rating or from the adjudicator’s own view.

## Permitted case closures

### Closed without rerating

Every operative route is resolved through object-level review, and neither initial rater changes an accepted rating.

### Closed after rerating

At least one original rater independently accepts an object-level consideration and submits an operator-assigned, predecessor-linked append-only rerating. The adjudicator cannot create or edit the rerating.

### Closed unresolved

At least one operative route remains unresolved. The record preserves the residual disagreement, minority interpretation, or evidential uncertainty. This is a valid closure and is not treated as adjudication failure.

Every closure must explain each operative route, preserve both initial ratings, include any accepted case reratings, acknowledge that scores were not imposed and convergence was not required, and receive independent quality control from an approved operator who is not the adjudicator.

## Rater discussion and rerating

Rater discussion is not automatically authorized by case opening. When Q-006B and Q-006C permit a discussion process, the operator separately authorizes it and controls what information becomes visible.

A valid rerating must be:

- authored by one of the original two raters;
- operator-assigned;
- linked to the prior accepted rating;
- locked after case opening;
- based on an explicit object-level revision reason; and
- stored without deleting or modifying the predecessor.

Operational correction of an unaccepted submission is not a philosophical rerating. A philosophical rerating is not a clerical correction.

## Final label snapshot

The final snapshot records the initial and latest-accepted rating distributions, accepted case closures, and unresolved-case flags. It does not create a replacement consensus score.

Both dedicated adjudicators sign the exact snapshot-body hash once each. Each sign-off confirms:

- the required cases are represented completely;
- original initial ratings remain preserved;
- residual disagreement is not hidden;
- no consensus score has been imposed; and
- participation does not imply endorsement of Metaphilosophy, the positions, the final labels, a funding application, or Phase 2.

Each sign-off receives independent quality control.

## Limited honoraria

The current plan reserves **USD 100** for accepted adjudication units.

One candidate unit may be generated by:

- an accepted adjudication record that closes an operator-assigned required case; or
- an accepted required final label-snapshot sign-off.

A rejected or superseded draft record, optional commentary, duplicate record, unassigned case, self-initiated work, or unaccepted sign-off earns zero units.

Candidate unit events do not authorize payment. The final accepted-unit ledger, identity and administrative checks, largest-remainder calculation, and separate payment authorization remain necessary. Unused reserve funds remain unspent.

Because the number and complexity of cases are unknown before initial ratings, no individual payout or time commitment can be promised. Before acceptance, the candidate must receive the frozen policy, expected maximum case load, item-length information, delivery window, payment limitations, and withdrawal procedure.

## Withdrawal, replacement, and conflicts discovered later

An adjudicator may withdraw. Accepted units already earned are not forfeited solely because of withdrawal.

A newly discovered conflict, prior exposure, or competence gap pauses the affected case. The case is reassigned only to an eligible adjudicator through a versioned controlled assignment. An existing resolution draft is not transferred as though it were an independent judgment; its status and exposure consequences are recorded.

If no eligible replacement exists, the case remains open and the limitation is disclosed. The project does not invent a closure to preserve schedule targets.

## Privacy and publication

Case packets, resolution drafts, object-level notes, rater identities, item IDs, ratings, sign-offs, and administrative records remain private controlled records.

Public reporting may include aggregate case counts, resolved and unresolved counts, workload balance, and cryptographic commitments. It must not reveal adjudicator, rater, item, rating, case, resolution, sign-off, or operator identifiers or protected philosophical content.

Any public naming, affiliation, quotation, or attribution requires separate permission. Participation is not endorsement.
