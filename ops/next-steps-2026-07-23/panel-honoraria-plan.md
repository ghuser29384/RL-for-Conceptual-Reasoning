# Hard Set expert panel and honoraria plan

Approved through 2026-07-25.

## Approved structure

- Panel: **6 core raters and 2 dedicated adjudicators**.
- Initial Hard Set workload: **800 blind ratings** across 400 critiques, with two independent ratings per critique.
- Delivery window: **4 weeks / 28 days**, measured from a passed readiness gate to the completion gate.
- Budget ceiling: **USD 500** in limited honoraria for volunteer expert work.
- Pool envelopes: **USD 400 core-rater completion pool** and **USD 100 adjudication reserve**.
- Human operations owner: **Ellen Sun, project owner**.

The USD 500 ceiling is not represented as covering the full labour cost of the programme. The LMCA paper estimates about 5–15 minutes for a short position–critique rating, implying about 66.7–200 hours for the 800 initial ratings alone. This excludes calibration, pairwise judgments, disagreement discussion, adjudication, administration, and replacement work.

## Contribution units

### Core-rater pool

One accepted blind initial rating earns one unit. One accepted, operator-assigned substantive re-rating earns one unit. Calibration, duplicates, rejected work, unassigned re-ratings, and resubmissions needed only to correct the contributor's own avoidable omission earn zero units.

Every accepted unit participates after the contributor has passed qualification, consent, conflict/exposure checks, calibration, and frozen quality control. There is no 80% or full-assignment threshold. This avoids denying an honorarium for accepted work when a contributor later withdraws or is replaced.

At normal completion, the USD 400 pool is divided pro rata by accepted units. At an owner-approved early closure, only the fraction `accepted blind initial ratings / 800` is released, capped at 1. Substantive re-rating units share the released amount but do not increase that release fraction. Whole-cent rounding uses the largest-remainder method with a stable contributor-ID tiebreak.

### Adjudication reserve

One accepted record closing an operator-assigned required adjudication case earns one unit. One accepted required final label-snapshot sign-off earns one unit. Administrative comments, duplicates, rejected records, unassigned reviews, and rework needed only to correct the adjudicator's own avoidable omission earn zero units.

At normal completion, the USD 100 reserve is divided pro rata by accepted units. At an owner-approved early closure, the released fraction is accepted adjudication units divided by opened adjudication-obligation units, capped at 1. An obligation unit is one assigned required case record or one required final sign-off assignment.

Unused adjudication-reserve funds remain unspent. They do not transfer to the core-rater pool.

## Start and completion rules

The absolute start date remains unset until readiness passes. Ellen Sun signs the readiness record. The start is the earliest Monday at 00:00 UTC that is at least 72 hours after that sign-off; the end is exactly 28 days later.

The readiness gate requires the exact controlled corpus, frozen Rubric v2 and adjudication rules, confirmed and calibrated panel members, blind assignments, and completed payment/legal readiness. The completion gate requires all 800 initial ratings, required disagreement handling, both final adjudicator sign-offs, and complete audit records.

## Assignment, attrition, and payment administration

A missed individual checkpoint triggers immediate notice and a request for a recovery or withdrawal update. After 48 hours without an approved recovery plan, new assignments pause and overdue unstarted work may be reassigned. Accepted units remain credited. Any replacement must pass the same qualification, consent, conflict, calibration, and exposure gates.

The target is to freeze the final accepted-unit ledger within five business days after the completion gate and disburse honoraria within fourteen calendar days after ledger freeze. These targets are contingent on payment onboarding and applicable legal, tax, sanctions, and jurisdiction checks.
