# Metaphilosophy public editorial voice

**Owner:** Ellen Sun  
**Applies to:** the public homepage, study plan, synthetic library, closed-intake page, workspace gate, metadata, and future public research reports.

Metaphilosophy should sound like a named researcher explaining a specific study. It should not sound like a software company describing a platform, an AI assistant arranging information into polished templates, or an internal compliance document published by accident.

## Voice

Write in plain, exact English. Prefer a concrete noun and verb over a slogan. Name the source of a claim. Name the person responsible for the project. Say what happened, what has not happened, and what remains to be decided.

Good:

> Metaphilosophy is a research project by Ellen Sun. The first study has not begun.

Avoid:

> Metaphilosophy is building a durable evidence layer for conceptual reasoning.

Good:

> Two philosophers would rate every critique independently.

Avoid:

> The protocol operationalizes multi-rater human judgment through a robust evidence pipeline.

## Structure

- Use headings that state the substantive point, not generic labels such as “Why this matters” or “How it works.”
- Do not repeat the same disclaimer in every section. Put each fact where a reader would naturally need it.
- Avoid three-card or six-card symmetry when the subject does not genuinely have three or six parts.
- Use numbered steps only for a real sequence.
- Keep internal gate codes, authorization language, cryptographic implementation details, and incident terminology out of public explanatory copy unless the reader needs them.
- Preserve technical precision in the study plan, but explain the consequence before the mechanism.

## Claims

- Distinguish the LMCA dataset, the Metaphilosophy synthetic library, and future Metaphilosophy research ratings.
- Attribute the LMCA counts and method to Cooper et al.
- Describe the synthetic library as model-written and unrated.
- State that Metaphilosophy has collected zero research ratings until that changes.
- Do not imply that a protected automated rehearsal is a completed human study.
- Do not use the possibility of future impact as evidence that the present work has succeeded.

## Phrases excluded from public copy

The following phrases are either generic AI diction, platform marketing, or internal process language. They should not appear on public pages:

- evidence layer
- artifact class or artifact classes
- truth before scale
- fail closed
- what exists—and what does not
- pre-outreach quality gate
- current operating status
- one gate passed
- five remain blocked
- human judgment infrastructure
- robust, seamless, transformative, cutting-edge, or revolutionary
- leverage, unlock, empower, foster, or navigate the complexities
- in today’s rapidly changing landscape

A term may still appear in internal engineering or operations documents where it has a precise technical meaning.

## Attribution and revision

The homepage and study plan identify Ellen Sun. The study plan includes a revision date. Substantive public claims should be traceable to the LMCA paper, the published synthetic release, or a versioned Metaphilosophy study record.

Before production promotion, public copy receives:

1. a factual/source check;
2. a sentence-level editorial pass;
3. an automated excluded-phrase check;
4. desktop and mobile rendered review; and
5. final owner review.
