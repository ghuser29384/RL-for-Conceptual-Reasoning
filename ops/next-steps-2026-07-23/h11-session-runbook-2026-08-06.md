## 2. Independence and role allocation

After both primary participants accept, assign the two journeys by a recorded random draw:

- **Journey A:** desktop, deliberate close/reopen recovery, final submission, then correction request.
- **Journey B:** narrow/mobile viewport, controlled failed request and retry, final submission, then withdrawal request.

Do not assign journeys based on anticipated technical ability or expected rating behavior.

Each participant must remain blind to:

- the other participant’s identity and participation;
- peer ratings, drafts, rationales, and debrief answers;
- hidden source or author information;
- model predictions, provisional labels, and adjudication state.

## 4. Participant task sequence

### Access and orientation

- Redeem the invitation.
- Confirm that the workspace displays only the assigned synthetic packet.
- Ask the participant to identify the synthetic-only notice and the privacy boundary.
- Confirm that all four critiques relate to the same position and that source/peer state is absent.

### Rubric comprehension before rating

Ask the participant to explain, in their own words:

1. centrality;
2. strength;
3. the significance of `strength × centrality`;
4. correctness;
5. clarity and the low-clarity branch;
6. dead weight;
7. single issue; and
8. overall quality.

The operator may clarify interface wording but must not suggest scores.

### Drafting and recovery

- Enter all required fields for each critique.
- Observe autosave state and version.
- Execute the journey-specific recovery event:
  - Journey A: close the tab or browser, then reopen and resume.
  - Journey B: trigger the approved transient request failure, then retry once.
- Verify no field is lost or duplicated.
- Verify stale-version behavior if the controlled script requires it.

### Submission

- Review the packet commitment and locked-state notice.
- Submit once.
- Verify the immutable receipt and exact assignment status.
- Verify that an immediate duplicate action is idempotent or rejected as designed.
- Do not manually edit accepted records.

### Post-submission path

- Journey A submits a correction request and observes the rater-visible status.
- Journey B submits a withdrawal request and observes the rater-visible status.
- The operator performs only the pre-approved response.
- Original initial records remain immutable.

## 5. Debrief

Collect answers without leading the participant:

1. What is the difference between centrality and strength?
2. Why is their product important?
3. What should happen when clarity is below 0.5?
4. Can an initial rating be overwritten?
5. What information was intentionally hidden?
6. What does the submission receipt prove?
7. What would you do after noticing a mistake?
8. What would you do if you wanted to withdraw?
9. Which interface states or instructions were confusing?
10. Did any step create a security, privacy, or data-integrity concern?
11. Was the stated 60–90 minute expectation accurate?
12. What one change would most reduce avoidable error?

Record exact substantive answers, not merely pass/fail labels.

## 6. Pass criteria

H-11 passes only if both participants:

- complete the assigned journey without operator-side record editing;
- correctly explain centrality, strength, and their product;
- understand the low-clarity rule;
- understand independent and immutable initial ratings;
- complete all required fields;
- resume with no lost work;
- receive and understand a submission receipt;
- complete the assigned correction or withdrawal path;
- report no unresolved P0 or P1 defect; and
- consent to the approved private evidence retention.

A P2 issue requires an owner-approved workaround or deferral.

## 7. Incident severity

- **P0:** credible disclosure of protected credentials or cross-account data; corruption or mutation of accepted records; non-synthetic or research-authorized content appears.
- **P1:** participant cannot access or complete the core path; draft loss; incorrect receipt; blinding failure; material rubric misunderstanding caused by the interface.
- **P2:** important but bounded usability defect with a safe documented workaround.
- **P3:** cosmetic or non-blocking improvement.

For P0/P1: stop, revoke, preserve evidence, repair, reverify the exact release, and start a fresh H-11 run. If the participant has already started and the stop is caused by a platform defect, security stop, or operator-required termination, record the full USD 75 as payable subject to recipient-specific payment checks.
