Draft study plan · research ratings have not begun

Written and maintained by Ellen Sun · revised 6 August 2026

A small test of whether philosophers can rate critiques consistently enough to be useful.

The study adapts the method in A dataset of rated conceptual arguments. A philosopher sees a position and several critiques of it, then rates each critique on seven specified dimensions. The aim is not to settle the underlying philosophical question. It is to find out whether the judgments themselves can be collected clearly, independently, and with an honest record of disagreement.

02 · Study design

One position is the unit of work.

Each rater receives complete groups of sibling critiques. This preserves the context needed to compare objections to the same position.

01

Twelve positions

The proposed set covers six philosophical topic families, with two positions in each. Every position has four critiques.

02

Two blind first ratings

Two philosophers rate every critique independently. They do not see the source, author or model, provisional labels, or the other rater’s judgment.

03

Balanced assignments

Six raters would each receive four positions. Topic competence, conflicts, and prior exposure are exclusions, not preferences that an operator may waive.

04

Permanent first records

If a later discussion changes someone’s view, the new rating is linked to the first one. It does not replace it.

05

Limited adjudication

An adjudicator can identify considerations, request a new object-level judgment, or record an unresolved case. An adjudicator cannot manufacture a compromise score.

06

Position-level analysis

The study has only twelve clusters. Results will therefore show the individual positions and leave-one-position-out sensitivity, not just one pooled statistic.

03 · The rubric

Seven separate judgments about each critique.

The labels below come from LMCA. Their detailed definitions matter; a familiar word such as “strength” is not enough on its own.

  1. 01

    Centrality

    If the attacked claim were false, how much would that damage the position?

  2. 02

    Strength

    How far does the critique establish the objection it actually makes?

  3. 03

    Correctness

    How much of the critique is true rather than false or misleading?

  4. 04

    Clarity

    After serious effort, can the critique’s meaning be fixed precisely enough to rate?

  5. 05

    Dead weight

    What material neither argues, clarifies, nor supplies relevant evidence?

  6. 06

    Single issue

    Does the critique pursue one objection or several independent ones?

  7. 07

    Overall

    How good is the critique once its force, precision, truth, and economy are considered together?

Two points that are easy to miss

Centrality and strength can trade off, so their product is the more stable estimate of a critique’s effect on the position. If clarity is below 0.5, the remaining component scores are much less reliable.

04 · What Metaphilosophy adds

Operational controls around the LMCA method.

These controls concern access, assignment, record integrity, and reporting. They do not change the substantive rating questions.

Candidate records

Before selection, the project records length, formatting, citation cues, source, model or author identity, attack family, and model-judge disagreement. These are selection diagnostics, never human labels.

Private rating packets

Each rater sees the text and rubric needed to judge the critique, but not provenance, another rater’s identity, aggregate scores, or adjudication state.

Exact submissions

Every submission is bound to the item versions and rubric the rater saw. Duplicate, missing, altered, and replayed submissions are rejected.

No silent editing

Quality control may accept or reject a response. It cannot rewrite a philosopher’s score or rationale.

Unresolved disagreement

The final record can contain more than one defensible judgment. The analysis will not conceal that fact by inventing consensus.

A separate decision about scale

Even a successful study would not automatically authorize a larger dataset. That would require new evidence, funding or committed capacity, and a separate decision.

06 · Limits

What a twelve-position study cannot show.

  • It cannot establish an objective answer to a philosophical question.
  • It cannot show that forty-eight critiques are enough to improve a model materially.
  • It cannot support a public ranking of the participating philosophers.
  • It cannot establish that one topic, source class, model family, or rater type is generally superior.
  • It cannot make the LMCA dataset, the synthetic library, and the new ratings interchangeable.
  • It cannot authorize a larger study without a new decision.
